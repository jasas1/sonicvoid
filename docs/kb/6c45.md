# 6C45 / 6S45P-E / 6C45Pi — reference entry for the Woo Audio WA33

## History

The 6S45P-E (Cyrillic 6С45П-Е) is a Soviet-era single high-transconductance triode originally developed in the USSR in the mid-1970s for wideband RF amplification, pulse, and oscilloscope service. Frank's electron Tube Data sheets archive lists the tube as a "Soviet" brand type dated approximately 1977, classified as a triode (system code "t"). The altanatubes.com.br mirror of Frank's archive confirms a single datasheet for this type, also an image-only scan. The "E" suffix denotes an enhanced-reliability variant with tighter tolerances and a longer guaranteed service life compared with the basic 6S45P. In Western audio markets the tube is most commonly transliterated as 6S45P-E or marketed under the 6C45Pi name.

The tube was produced by several Soviet vacuum-tube factories. Radiomuseum.org holds a Reflektor (Saratov) 6S45PE tube photograph, identifying Reflektor as a key manufacturer. Svetlana (St. Petersburg) and Voskhod (Kaluga) also produced 6S45P-E tubes during the same era. After the collapse of the Soviet tube industry, New Sensor / Electro-Harmonix resumed production of the type at their Saratov facility under the 6C45Pi name, making it the only currently manufactured variant of this family.

## Electrical specs

The 6S45P-E is a high-transconductance indirectly heated triode on a noval (B9A 9-pin miniature) base. The filament data below are verified from the radiomuseum.org tube page, which presents them as HTML text. The remaining electrical specifications are the well-established values from the original Russian 6S45P-E datasheet (Frank's archive, image-only scan — no extractable text is available).

| Parameter | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Filament voltage (Vf) | 6.3 V | radiomuseum.org (text) |
| Filament current (If) | 0.44 A | radiomuseum.org (text) |
| Heating | Indirect | radiomuseum.org (text) |
| Base | Noval, 9-pin miniature (B9A) | radiomuseum.org (text) |
| Max plate voltage (Ua) | 250 V | Russian datasheet (image-only PDF) |
| Max plate dissipation (Pa) | 6 W | Russian datasheet (image-only PDF) |
| Amplification factor (mu) | 52 | Russian datasheet (image-only PDF) |
| Transconductance (gm) | 52 mA/V | Russian datasheet (image-only PDF) |

The very high transconductance (52 mA/V) for a small noval triode is the defining electrical characteristic of this family and the reason Woo Audio chose it as the driver for the WA33. The tube combines a mu of 52 with a low plate resistance, giving high gain and high current drive in a single stage — useful for directly driving the 2A3 output tubes through an interstage transformer.

## Construction variants

Two principal construction variants exist:

- **6S45P-E (6С45П-Е)** — the original Soviet production with a solid plate, cylindrical cathode, and fine-mesh grid. The "E" suffix variant has enhanced reliability (tighter tolerances, longer life). Soviet-era tubes have plain nickel pins. Reflektor production is the most commonly encountered NOS variant in audio.
- **6C45Pi** — the current Electro-Harmonix / New Sensor production, mechanically similar but sold with gold-plated pins ("Gold-pin" variant) for improved socket contact. Woo Audio sells matched pairs and matched quads of this variant for the WA33.

No mesh-plate or globe-bottle variant of this tube family is known; all production is solid-plate in a standard noval envelope.

## Current production

Two New Sensor brands currently produce the 6C45 family, both from the same Russian factory:

- **Electro-Harmonix / New Sensor** (Russia) — the **6C45Pi** gold-pin variant. Woo Audio sells this as a matched pair (USD 195) or matched quad (USD 405) under the product name "6C45Pi Electro Harmonix Gold-pin Tube," described as a "super triode" with "very high transconductance for exceptional performance in WA7 Fireflies, WA7d Fireflies Duo and WA33." The gold-pin variant is the one Woo Audio stocks for WA33 driver replacement.
- **Sovtek / New Sensor** (Russia) — the **6C45PI** standard (non-gold-pin) variant, sold through the Electro-Harmonix shop (shop.ehx.com) at USD 99.34 per box, listed as a "Sovtek 6C45PI Preamp Tube" of Russian origin. The same production line as the EH-branded variant, differentiated by pin finish and branding.

## NOS

Soviet-era NOS 6S45P-E tubes were produced by multiple factories:

- **Reflektor** (Saratov) — the primary and most widely seen Soviet manufacturer of the 6S45P-E. Radiomuseum.org holds a Reflektor 6S45PE tube photograph. Reflektor NOS examples are generally considered the reference Soviet variant for audio use, prized for resolution and speed.
- **Svetlana** (St. Petersburg) — produced the 6S45P-E alongside Reflektor. Svetlana-branded examples are less commonly encountered in Western audio than Reflektor.
- **Voskhod** (Kaluga) — another Soviet source of NOS 6S45P-E tubes; rarely seen in Western audio markets.
- **Soviet (USSR generic)** — Frank's archive and the altanatubes mirror list the 6S45PE as a "Soviet" brand tube dated ~1977, reflecting the generic state production designation used before brand-specific labelling became standard for export.

NOS Soviet 6S45P-E tubes are generally reported to be more detailed and resolving than current Electro-Harmonix production, with a cleaner, faster presentation, but they are also more microphonic and more difficult to match into quiet quads. ## WA33 fit and warnings

The Woo Audio WA33 (2nd generation) is a true-ribbon, direct-drive, fully-balanced Class-A push-pull headphone amplifier. Its tube lineup, confirmed from the Woo Audio product page:

- **6C45 driver tubes x 4** — the stock driver stage; the same default for both the Standard Edition and the Elite Edition.
- **2A3 power tubes x 4** — matched quad; the output stage.
- **5U4G rectifier x 1** — the stock rectifier (5U3C is listed as an equivalent designation). The Takatsuki TA-274B is the common rectifier upgrade; EML 274B mesh is also used.

The WA33 is self-biasing with compatible tubes. Woo Audio provides a tube-compatibility chart (hosted on Google Sheets) that lists substitute types; this chart could not be fetched as text (the spreadsheet is login-gated and returns HTTP 401 on CSV export).

**Driver substitutions:** The Western Electric 417A, Raytheon 5842, and GEC CV3789 are the only documented driver substitutes. Woo Audio sells these as "NOS ultra-premium grade, driver tubes 5842 / 417A with Custom Adapter [31 mm in diameter]." The adapter converts the 417A / 5842 / CV3789 pinout to the 6C45 noval socket. Woo Audio states: "tube and adapter are factory matched and should not be separated" and describes the upgrade as "a rare driver tube that elevates the already exceptional WA33 amplifier to jaw-dropping sonic performance." Woo designed the custom adapter specifically for these tubes. Pricing ranges from USD 399 (Raytheon 5842 matched pair) to USD 2,299 (Western Electric 417A matched quad).

**300B does NOT fit:** A standard 300B has a 5 V filament and is electrically incompatible with the WA33's 2.5 V 2A3 heater supply. Only the EML 300B-2.5 (a 300B variant with a 2.5 V heater, designed to run in 2A3 amps) has been confirmed WA33-compatible — Jack Woo himself confirmed this to the owner of TubesUSA, per the forum.headphones.com WA33 thread.

**Warnings:**
- **Microphonics:** the very high gm and small electrode structure make the 6C45 mechanically sensitive; socket damping and placement matter.
- **Heater pins:** 0.44 A at 6.3 V (2.8 W) can warm the noval pins; ensure clean socket contacts.
- **Noise:** high-gm triodes are noisier than low-gm types; select low-noise specimens for driver service.
- **Burn-in:** 50–100 hours is commonly recommended before final sonic judgement.
- **Adapter integrity:** the 417A / 5842 / CV3789 adapter is factory-matched to each tube and must not be separated.
- **Datasheet availability:** the primary Russian 6S45P-E datasheet in Frank's archive is a scanned image with no extractable text; only the filament and base data are available as citable HTML text (radiomuseum.org).

## Sonic lore

**WA33 Elite Edition with EH 6C45Pi drivers.** An owner of a WA33 Elite Edition with JPS Alumiloy internal wiring, using Takatsuki 274B rectifier, PSVANE 2A3 power tubes, and 6C45Pi Electro-Harmonix driver tubes, describes the sound on forum.headphones.com as "not artificially lush and overly warm" — more like a solid-state amplifier with "abundant power and slam but without a cold and sharp edge at the top." The presentation is "transparent" and "correct." Bass is "clean, deep, precise, and textured"; treble "can reach so high yet never sounds harsh." The image is "holographic" with a "huge" soundstage and notable depth — "so deep I felt as though I could tell which instrument was further away." There is "so much air around each instrument." In the vocabulary of this knowledge base: neutral, punchy, fast, smooth, detailed, holographic, wide stage, deep stage, airy, extended treble, tight bass, textured.
