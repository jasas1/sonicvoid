# 2A3 tube family for Woo Audio WA33

## History

The 2A3 is a directly heated audio power triode introduced by RCA in the early 1930s; Radiomuseum dates the RCA introduction to 1933-01-10. It became a classic American hi-fi output tube, strongly associated with push-pull radio and theatre practice, and later with low-power single-ended triode amplifiers. Early examples include single-plate and dual/bi-plate constructions; R-type describes an RCA example with two triode assemblies connected in parallel inside the bulb.

## Electrical specs

Reference RCA-family values for the classic 2A3 are: filament 2.5 V at 2.5 A, directly heated; UX4 four-pin base; maximum plate voltage 300 V; maximum plate dissipation 15 W; amplification factor about 4.2; plate resistance about 800 ohms; transconductance about 5.2 to 5.25 mA/V. Common class A1 single-ended reference operation is 250 V plate, -45 V grid, 60 mA plate current, 2500 ohm load, and about 3.5 W output. The saved RCA and Sylvania PDFs are image-only under `pdftotext`, so searchable evidence cites text mirrors and maker data while the scanned PDFs remain in `pages/`.

## Construction variants

Important variants are early single-plate, later bi-plate/dual-plate, ST bottle, globe-style collector or replica tubes, mesh-plate versions, and high-dissipation 2A3-40 or XLS-style variants. Mesh is a structure, not a rating: always check the maker's dissipation and handling rules. 6B4G is related electrically but uses a different base; 300B is a different tube and not a 2A3 substitute.

## Current production

Current or recent production sources include JJ 2A3-40, Electro-Harmonix 2A3EH, Sovtek/Reflektor 2A3, Sophia Electric mesh/globe 2A3, Psvane 2A3, Emission Labs 2A3 variants, Takatsuki TA-2A3, Elrog ER2A3 variants, KR Audio 2A3, Linlai 2A3, and Shuguang 2A3 variants. JJ's 2A3-40 is a high-dissipation tube with 2.5 V / 2.5 A filament, mu 4.2, 800 ohm Ri, and 40 W limiting anode dissipation; that does not make every WA33 operating point suitable for every 2A3-40-like tube.

## NOS

Main NOS families to track are RCA/Cunningham, Sylvania, Tung-Sol, Ken-Rad, National Union, GE, Raytheon, Philco, Westinghouse, and military VT-95/CV1831 relabels. Treat the construction and factory code as more important than the carton: many consumer brands reboxed tubes made by the large factories. Single-plate RCA/Sylvania-style examples and clean early dual-plate tubes usually carry the strongest collector premium.

## WA33 fit and warnings

The WA33 2nd generation uses four 2A3 power tubes, four 6C45 drivers, and one rectifier; Woo's page lists the default rectifier as 5U4G and describes the amplifier as fully balanced push-pull class A. Buy 2A3s as a matched quad. Do not install 300B tubes. Be cautious with adapters, tall globe bottles, mesh filaments, thoriated-tungsten variants, and high-dissipation 2A3-40 tubes unless Woo or the tube maker confirms heater draw, bias, clearance, and socket compatibility.

## Sonic lore

Classic RCA and Sylvania 2A3s are usually described as smooth, rich, textured, and intimate rather than high-power or aggressive. Sovtek/Reflektor 2A3 has a reputation for surprisingly strong value and directness; Glow in the Dark Audio calls an early-2000s Sovtek the best sounding tube in its small comparison. Sophia mesh/globe and Psvane-style audiophile tubes are often discussed for airy, romantic presentation, while JJ 2A3-40 is treated as robust and dynamic but not a pure vintage clone.
